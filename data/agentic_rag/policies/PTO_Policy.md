# Paid Time Off (PTO) Policy
**Version:** 2.0
**Effective Date:** January 1, 2024
**Last Updated:** January 1, 2024

## Overview
This policy outlines the company's Paid Time Off (PTO) program, which combines vacation, sick leave, and personal days into a single flexible time-off allowance.

## Eligibility
All full-time employees are eligible for PTO benefits after completion of their 30-day probationary period.

## Annual PTO Allocation

### By Tenure
- **Entry Level (0-2 years):** 15 PTO days per fiscal year
- **Mid Level (2-5 years):** 20 PTO days per fiscal year
- **Senior Level (5+ years):** 25 PTO days per fiscal year
- **Lead/Manager:** 25 PTO days per fiscal year

### Accrual
- PTO accrues on a monthly basis (1/12 of annual allocation each month)
- Employees can request PTO after it has accrued
- Part-time employees receive pro-rated PTO based on hours worked

## Usage Guidelines

### Request and Approval
- Employees must submit PTO requests at least **2 weeks in advance** for planned time off
- Emergency or unplanned leave should be notified to manager as soon as possible
- Managers are expected to approve/deny requests within 3 business days
- Requests are approved on a first-come, first-served basis

### Blackout Dates
- No more than 2 consecutive weeks can be approved during Q4 (October-December)
- Department coverage must be maintained at all times
- Manager may request alternative dates if business needs require

## Carryover and Payout

### Carryover Rules
- Employees may carry over up to **5 days** into the next fiscal year
- Carryover days must be used within the first 6 months of the new fiscal year
- Days exceeding 5 days are forfeited (use-it-or-lose-it policy)
- Carryover does not accumulate beyond one fiscal year

### Separation
- Upon termination, employees are paid out for all accrued, unused PTO at their regular rate of pay
- Accrued PTO up to the date of separation is paid in the final paycheck
- Company may require use of PTO during notice period before separation

## Special Circumstances

### Holidays
- Company holidays are paid time off and do not count against PTO balance
- If a holiday falls during an employee's scheduled PTO, the holiday is paid and PTO is not deducted

### Illness
- Employees should use PTO for illness and medical appointments
- Extended absences due to serious illness or disability may qualify for short-term or long-term disability instead
- Medical certification may be requested for absences exceeding 3 consecutive days

### Jury Duty and Court Appearances
- Employees called for jury duty will be paid for time served (up to standard work hours)
- Court-ordered appearances are also paid time off
- Employees must provide court documentation

## Remote Work During PTO
- PTO is intended for rest and recovery; employees are not expected to work during PTO
- Checking emails or performing work tasks during approved PTO is not permitted
- Emergency situations requiring work during PTO should be addressed with manager approval in advance

## Manager Responsibilities
- Maintain accurate PTO balance records for direct reports
- Approve/deny requests in timely manner with clear communication
- Plan team coverage to ensure all requests can be accommodated
- Monitor PTO usage to ensure employees are taking adequate time off
- Remind employees about carryover deadlines to prevent forfeiture

## Policy Exceptions
- CEO and executives may have different PTO policies
- Sales positions may have usage restrictions during high-volume periods
- Employees on sabbatical, extended medical leave, or unpaid leave have PTO suspension

## Questions and Clarification
Contact HR at hr@company.com for PTO-related questions or to report issues with approvals.
